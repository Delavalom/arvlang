# Phase 6x7: review round 1

This file continues [`16zb-p67-notes.md`](16zb-p67-notes.md). Review round 1 had two Critical findings and seven Minor ones. Each Critical finding has a regression test that failed before its fix, for the reason the finding gives.

## Fixes

**A resume overspent `tokens` and `usd_cents` (Critical).** In a resume, a task past its records goes live at once, even while other tasks still have logged `llm_reserve` records to act out. Its live reservation read a pool that did not yet hold those reservations, so `Budget.Reserve` could grant it. The logged reservations were then forced through by `ReserveLogged`, which never refuses. The level ended below zero and no error was raised. The money was really spent: the crashed run had paid for the replayed calls, and the resume paid for the live call on top. The reviewer measured this under `tokens: 7000` (and `usd_cents: 7`): the recording answers a (4000 tokens) and refuses b, but the resume answered b live, so 8500 tokens or 8.5 cents were spent, with output that differed from the uninterrupted run.

Now a live model-budget step in a resume waits until every logged reservation has been acted out (`Task.AwaitReservations`, `arv/runtime/budget_llm_order.go`). The live steps are a reservation, a settlement past a reservation, and a call that a replayed grant sends live. This follows the precedent of a live failure, which waits for the log to drain (`Task.Fail`). Every logged record happened before the crash, so it happened before any live step. The log counts its `llm_reserve` records when it is read (`countModels`). A logged reservation counts as acted out:

- when it is settled, if it was granted (its call's usage is then in the pools, including what an unlogged settlement within the reservation gives back);
- when it is replayed, if it was refused;
- when it is replayed, if it was granted and is its task's last record. Its call is then sent live.

The wait parks, so the stall watcher still turns a forged log that never lets a reservation be acted out into a divergence rather than a hang. It is `idle`, so it takes no time on the recorded run's clock.

Test: `TestResumeWaitsForLoggedReservations` (the reviewer's probe, for `tokens: 7000` and `usd_cents: 7`, resumed three times each, with a goroutine leak check). Before the fix it printed `"a answered\nb answered\n…"` and spent 8500 tokens.

**A forged trailing grant sent a call past a scope's limit (Critical).** If a granted `llm_reserve` is a task's last record, the call after it runs live. `ReserveLogged` takes the reservation whatever the pool holds, and the settlement then fits inside the reservation, so nobody noticed. A log edited to turn a refused reservation into a granted one (the hash chain is not a signature) sent one call of 4000 tokens under `scope (tokens: 1000)`, and the run ended well. Scope limits are written in the program, not in the header, so this was outside the forged-header limit of `16q`/`16y`. Now such a grant waits for the logged reservations, as above. Then, if any `tokens` or `usd_cents` level at or above the call's is below zero (`Budget.Overdrawn`), the resume diverges ("the log grants a reservation of … which the budget cannot cover") before anything is sent. Tests: `TestResumeRefusesAForgedTrailingGrant` sent one call before the fix. It now diverges with no provider call. `TestResumeSendsAnInFlightGrantedCall` checks that a genuine in-flight grant is still sent again.

## Known limits (round 1)

These replace the first and third bullets of `16zb`'s Known limits.

- **A live model step in a resume waits for every logged reservation, even one in an unrelated budget.** The cost is ordering, not results: a live task can wait for replayed tasks whose reservations it could never have raced. A forged log whose reservation is never acted out stalls the resume until the stall watcher diverges (`16w`).
- **A replayed trailing grant is checked against the whole level, not against what was free when it was granted.** After the wait, a level below zero makes the resume diverge. Two cases make an honest log diverge here. (1) A sibling's settlement past its reservation went over the limit (a budget error, logged as such) after the grant and before the crash, while the granted call was in flight. The uninterrupted run would have sent the call. The resume refuses it, which errs on the side of the limit. (2) A live call that a resumed task reserved after the wait settles past its reservation and over the limit before the trailing task checks. This is a window of microseconds against the length of a model call. Both cases need an overspent level, which only a settlement past its reservation can cause.
- **A forged log can still change what a resume's live part may spend** by dropping a call's `llm_reserve` and `llm` records, or by lifting a run-level limit in the header (`16q`, `16y`). A forged grant that is its task's last record is now checked as above. A forged grant followed by a forged `llm` record sends nothing, because the call is replayed.
- **Uncharged (Minor, a constant per call): about 3.1 KB allocated per limited model call, none of it retained.** Each limited call encodes its two integers, builds an `llm_reserve` record (and an `llm_settle` record when it settles past its reservation), marshals it and hashes it. The log line is about 144 bytes. Measured with `runtime.MemStats.TotalAlloc` over 400 recorded calls under `tokens: 10^9`, against the same 400 calls under `steps: 10^9` (best of 5): 4 972 960 B against 3 722 576 B, so 3126 B per call. Nothing grows with input the script controls beyond the call count, which the budgets bound.
- **Whether the concurrent-budget tests catch a regression depends on the replay's CPU timing.** `TestReplayOfConcurrentModelBudgets`, `TestReplayOfConcurrentModelSettlements` and `TestResumeWaitsForLoggedReservations` rely on a's replayed `spin()` (about 300 ms of CPU) to let b reach the budget first. With the fixes they pass in either order, so they cannot flake. On a heavily loaded machine, though, the replay may not reorder, and a regression could pass unnoticed in that run. Each test runs its replay or resume three times to lower that chance. No test asserts the reorder, because that would need a hook in the evaluator only for tests.
- **A reply whose `Block.Raw` is not valid JSON fails when recorded** ("the reply cannot be logged") and succeeds unrecorded. This is deliberate (`16u`): the log cannot hold it, and a replay must read back what the recording went on with.
